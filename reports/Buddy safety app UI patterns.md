# Safety apps ask first and alert contacts after silence

Popular buddy and trusted-contact safety systems share one skeleton: the person arms a time-boxed commitment, the app prompts that person privately when something looks off or a timer ends, and contacts are told only if the person stays silent. Apple's Check In is the best-documented and closest analogue to YTC ("Your Trusted Companion"). It has a visible active state, pre-chosen data tiers, extend-by-15/30/60-minute prompts, and automatic notification of a contact on silence ([Apple Personal Safety guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/ips56b5bc469/web)). Rideshare apps (Uber, Lyft, Bolt) add a persistent safety entry point and a split between live-share contacts and contact-if-unreachable contacts. Campus tools (Rave Guardian) add guardians who need no app, and location revealed only on expiry or duress. The notes found no surveyed app that combines a group map, per-participant consent, a host, backup-contact escalation, acknowledgment separate from resolution, and a labelled demo mode. Those are YTC's novel areas, and they are also where the evidence is thinnest. Most findings are desk research from help pages and search summaries, not screenshots or user studies. Every timing (10 s, 15 s, 30 s, 60 s, 15 min) is a vendor default with no evidence that it is optimal. Section labels below separate sourced findings from inferences, and the final sections list unverified items and gaps.

## The shared pattern: arm, prompt privately, notify on silence

The strongest regularity across apps is the order of operations. Apple Check In has a timer mode and a "When I arrive" mode. At timer end the user is prompted to end the check-in or add 15, 30 or 60 minutes, and the contact sees the updated end time. If the phone is not moving toward the destination, the user gets a prompt with **15 minutes to respond** ([Apple Personal Safety guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/ips56b5bc469/web)). The contact receives a link to shared information automatically only when the check-in does not end as expected: no arrival, significant delay with no response, an SOS call with no follow-up response, no response at timer end, or a lost phone. If the user answers in time, the partner learns only that the user checked in ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2023/10/09/ios-17s-check-in-feature-provides-peace-of-mind-and-could-even-save-lives/)). Google's Pixel Safety Check works the same way. If the user does not respond before the timer ends, Emergency Sharing starts and contacts get a text with a Maps link ([Google Support](https://support.google.com/assistant/answer/13683607?hl=en)). Kitestring (SMS, circa 2014) texts the user at trip end and alerts contacts if there is no reply ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/16/kitestring/)). Companion (2015-16) showed a big green YES button after detected anomalies such as running, pulled-out headphones, a phone drop or late arrival, reportedly with a 15-second window, and its contacts needed no app, only a text link to a map ([Fox29](https://fox29.com/news/new-personal-safety-app-provides-virtual-companions), [Google Cloud blog 2016](https://cloud.googleblog.com/2016/04/never-walk-home-alone-again-with-Companion-app-and-Google-Maps.html?version=v1), [NIJ](https://nij.ojp.gov/index.php/library/publications/companion-see-you-safely-home)).

Rideshare apps use the same ask-first logic but anchor it in a persistent entry point. Uber has a shield button on the trip screen that opens the Safety Toolkit ([Uber US](https://www.uber.com/us/en/ride/safety/)), and Lyft has a "Safety tools" overlay between pickup and drop-off ([PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/story/diy/app-safety-features/)). Uber RideCheck uses sensors and GPS to detect a possible crash, route deviation or unexpectedly long stop, then prompts the rider with help options ([Uber US](https://www.uber.com/us/en/ride/safety/), [Uber AU](https://www.uber.com/au/en/ride/safety/)). At its 2019 launch, Uber said machine learning screens out false positives such as a lost phone ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2019/09/17/132985/uber-will-use-your-smartphones-sensors-to-check-if-your-car-has-stopped-suddenly/amp/), [Android Central](https://androidcentral.com/uber-launches-ridecheck-detect-accidents-your-smartphone)). Uber keeps two contact tiers apart. Trusted Contacts receive real-time trip status the rider chose to share, while Emergency Contacts may be contacted only if Uber cannot reach the rider after a serious incident ([Uber US](https://www.uber.com/us/en/ride/safety/), [Uber JP](https://www.uber.com/jp/en/ride/safety/)). Lyft draws the same line ([Lyft safety page](https://www.lyft.com/driver/safety/insurance)). Dating apps move sharing before the event: Tinder Share My Date sends location, time and a match photo, scheduled up to 30 days ahead ([Stylist](https://www.stylist.co.uk/relationships/tinder-share-my-date/882151), [Fox 7](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/tinder-hinge-safety-feature-save-my-date-hidden-words)). Bumble Share Date lets users update contacts when plans change ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/17/bumble-heightens-safety-measures-with-new-id-verification-feature/)).

Other apps in the notes fill out the picture. bSafe offers Guardians, Follow Me GPS, a fake call, voice-activated SOS streaming and a timer alarm ([AppleVis](https://applevis.com/apps/ios/lifestyle/bsafe-never-walk-alone), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bsafe/id459709106), [WGN](https://digital-release.wgntv.com/?p=357931)). Circle of 6 uses six slots in a circle and one-tap preset messages, including "call to interrupt me", and texts each contact an explanation of their role when added ([Next City](https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/in-wake-of-new-delhi-rape-an-app-to-protect-against-sexual-violence-undergo), [Jewell Hilltop Monitor](https://hilltopmonitor.jewell.edu/the-circle-of-6-app-is-a-one-click-key-to-college-student-safety/)). Life360's SOS silently alerts the Circle with location, and its Crash Detection (collisions over 25 mph) prompts the user and alerts the Circle on no response ([Life360 investor release](https://investors.life360.com/news-releases/news-release-details/life360-enhances-family-safety-app-launch-new-features-and-0)). Citizen Protect sells 24/7 agents who can escalate to emergency services ([KQED](https://ww2.kqed.org/forum/2010101885164/citizen-app-adds-24-7-safety-experts-raising-questions-about-disaster-voyeurism-vigilantism)). Rave Guardian's Safety Timer lets the user pick a guardian (a contact, campus Public Safety, or both). Guardians are messaged automatically on expiry, and if Public Safety is the official guardian its dispatchers call the user and, if unreachable, send an officer to the last reported location ([UMN Public Safety](https://publicsafety.umn.edu/campus-safety/rave-guardian-campus-safety-app)). Other deployments reveal location only on expiry or duress and offer a duress PIN ([Wichita State](https://www.wichita.edu/about/wsunews/archive/index.php?nid=3429), [UAlbany](https://www.albany.edu/police/rave-guardian-personal-safety-app)).

Inference (not sourced): YTC's private check-in after a movement change takes its prompt from event-triggered systems and its human escalation target from scheduled check-in systems. Uber's two-tier contact model maps onto YTC's host versus backup contacts. No surveyed app has a recipient acknowledgment step, a backup tier, or acknowledgment separate from resolution, so that is a possible YTC differentiator that remains unvalidated.

## Countdowns, cancel gestures and the false-alarm tax

Event-triggered detection uses a two-stage prompt. Apple Watch Fall Detection taps the wrist, sounds an alarm and offers "I'm OK" or an Emergency Call slider. If the wearer is moving, it does not auto-call. After about a minute of immobility it starts a **30-second countdown** with a louder alert and wrist taps ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108896)). Apple Crash Detection shows an alert for 10 seconds, then a second countdown with loud sounds, aggressive vibration and a flashing LED ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104959)). The notes conflict on that second stage: one fetched summary says 30 s, and a search snippet says 10 s, so the length is unconfirmed. Pixel Car Crash Detection gives **60 seconds** and accepts voice ("Emergency" or "Cancel") or touch, and a paired watch counts as one response ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7055029?hl=en)). Garmin Edge gives 30 seconds to cancel with a confirm step ([Garmin Edge 130 Plus manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/edge130plus/EN-SG/GUID-AACEBE21-21E8-4D00-A536-3099D35B0928-4286.html)), and Garmin Drive reportedly uses 60 seconds ([Garmin Drive manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/driveluxe51/EN-US/GUID-DD2612E8-290D-47EC-9C78-07A3392DFE61.html)). Samsung offers a user-chosen countdown and an optional "Require swipe to call" ([O2 guide](https://www.o2.co.uk/help/phones-sims-and-devices/samsung/galaxy-watch-ultra-2025-wear-os-6/setup-and-first-use/turn-fall-detection-on-or-off), [EE guide](https://deviceguides.ee.co.uk/samsung/galaxy-watch7-android-wear-os/basic-functions/turning-fall-detection-on-or-off/)). Cancel methods mostly require the user to prove presence. Noonlight is a hold-to-arm button with a 10-second PIN window after release, and dispatchers try to contact the user first ([WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Guides/noonlight-panic-button-for-solo-travel-safety), [Noonlight Help](https://help.noonlight.com/en/articles/2060965-how-does-noonlight-work)). Bolt SOS requires a 3-second hold plus a Yes confirmation ([MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/business/business_news/126334/bolt_rolls_out_new_trip_safety_features), [Connecting Africa](https://www.connectingafrica.com/emerging-technology/bolt-introduces-additional-safety-features-on-driver-s-app)). Lyft Emergency Help lets the rider choose a text, a call, or a silent alert through ADT ([Lyft blog](https://www.lyft.com/blog/posts/lyft-launches-emergency-help), [GlobeNewswire 2020](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2020/11/18/2129467/0/en/ADT-Mobile-Safety-Powers-Lyft-s-New-Emergency-Help-Feature-in-Nationwide-Rollout.html)). Peoplesafe documents cancel-after-accidental-trigger options, adjustable sensitivity and visible countdowns ([Peoplesafe](https://nexussupport.peoplesafe.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/37056554520849-Understanding-Alarm-Triggers)).

Automatic triggering has a measurable cost. In Okayama City (Oct 2022 to Mar 2023), **35 of 49** crash-detection app calls to emergency services were false positives (71%), though the authors call the study preliminary and single-city ([Hongo et al., Acute Medicine & Surgery](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10363817)). Ski-region dispatchers reported floods of automated calls, and Apple said updates reduced them and users get 10 seconds to cancel; the specific counts came from search summaries only ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/01/16/crash-detection-false-alerts/)). Further reporting covers accidental Apple Watch SOS calls ([CTV Vancouver](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/apple-watch-sos-causes-false-alarm-for-rescuers-in-metro-vancouver/), [Panda Security](https://www.pandasecurity.com/en/mediacenter/android-sos-feature-problems/)). One further local-news source on dispatcher call volume was omitted because its URL slug contains an emergency number. Delhi's Himmat app cancels registration after three false SOS alerts, which is a deterrent but a blunt one ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Frajnath-singh-launches-women-safety-2104478)). The Kitestring author accepts that missed check-ins cause false alarms (dead phone, sleep) as better than nobody noticing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/16/kitestring/)). Apple's own page warns that a lost phone during Check In notifies the contact as if the user were unresponsive ([Apple Personal Safety guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/ips56b5bc469/web)). Clinical alarm-fatigue literature ties desensitization to excess false alarms, though the evidence is weak and does not necessarily transfer to friend groups ([Revista de Enfermagem UFPE](https://periodicos.ufpe.br/revistas/revistaenfermagem/article/view/11603), [Frontiers in Digital Health 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.843747/epub), [JMIR Med Inform 2020](https://medinform.jmir.org/2020/7/e15653/PDF)). A 2026 review found alert fatigue is measured inconsistently and recommends tracking sustained drops in appropriate-response rate ([Ray Wilson et al. 2026](https://www.metrohealth.org/globalassets/metrohealth-documents/population-health-research-institute/ray-wilson-et-al-2026-alert-fatigue-systematic-review.pdf)). Transport Scotland's review lists late messages, battery use and accidental triggers as common app problems ([Transport Scotland](https://transportscotland.gov.uk/publication/there-s-an-app-for-that-women-s-safety-on-public-transport-in-scotland/limitations-of-personal-safety-apps)).

Inference (not sourced): nightlife (dancing, crowds, jostling) resembles the high-impact contexts that trigger false crash alerts, so a private prompt before anyone is notified is the main lever. Movement-conditional gating, like Apple's immobility wait, is the main vendor false-alarm filter. Repeating an alert without new information risks desensitization, so each escalation step should send once and the host view should show "still unacknowledged since HH:MM" instead of new pings. Noonlight-style PIN cancellation may suit an impaired user poorly, so a simple large tap is preferable. Hold-to-send protects manual help from accidents, and a cancellation should leave a visible timeline entry and tell recipients rather than erase events. Silent duress signals have only patent descriptions as evidence ([US patent 9672727](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9672727), [Patsnap US20180033288A1](https://eureka.patsnap.com/patent-US20180033288A1), [US patent 10477008](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10477008), [US patent 10373478](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10373478)). A decoy "all fine" state would deceive the user's own contacts, so it should be optional, disclosed and validated before use.

## Consent, data tiers and the coercion problem

Consent in the surveyed apps is mostly sender-initiated. Google Maps location sharing is off by default, per-person notification permission is selectable, and viewers can see your battery status ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/7326816)). Life360 Circles scope visibility to a group, and Places adds arrival and departure alerts ([Wikipedia: Life360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life360)). Snap Map has a "Who Can See My Location" setting and shares nothing until the Map is first opened ([Snapchat Support](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012343074580-How-do-I-use-Ghost-Mode-on-Snap-Map)). Apple Check In is the only one with a pre-agreed **data tier**: Limited (current location, battery and signal) or Full (adds route traveled and last unlock and Watch-removal locations), chosen before any incident and sent only when a check-in ends unexpectedly ([Apple Personal Safety guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/ips56b5bc469/web)). Its Lock Screen shows only "Check In: Unlock to view details", which protects privacy, with cancel available after unlocking. Glympse recipients need no app and get a web link, and shares expire on a timer or arrival ([Glympse FAQ](https://support.glympse.com/faq/how-does-glympse-work/)). WhatsApp live location offers 15 minute, 1 hour and 8 hour durations, and a screen lists the chats you are currently sharing into (secondary sources only: [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/how-to-share-location-on-whatsapp/articleshow/68352516.cms), [AirDroid guide](https://airdroid.com/parent-control/how-to-send-live-location-on-whatsapp)). Life360 distinguishes a manual pause ("Location Paused") from lost connection or battery (an exclamation mark), per third-party sources ([AirDroid](https://www.airdroid.com/location-tracking/know-if-someone-delete-life360/)). Zenly's Ghost Mode let users freeze, blur or hide from specific friends before Snap shut the app down in February 2023, reportedly for profitability and not privacy ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2022/12/zenly-social-mapping-network-shutting-down-on-february-3rd-2023/), [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/snaps-decision-shut-down-zenly)).

Recipient-side consent is weak across the field. The notes found no strong accept flow beyond Circle of 6's role-explaining text, and Google's Safety Check and Kitestring pages do not say whether contacts consent ([Google Support](https://support.google.com/assistant/answer/13683607?hl=en), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/16/kitestring/)). That matters because location sharing between people who know each other is a documented coercion vector. Life360 responded to criticism with Bubbles (approximate location, 1 to 25 miles, 1 to 6 hours) and said it stopped selling precise location data ([Wikipedia: Life360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life360)), and the Washington Post, The Markup and Sky News coverage is relayed only through that page. An Australian eSafety survey of 2,046 adults found **13.6%** agreed it is reasonable to expect to track an intimate partner at any time ([eSafety](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/track-harass-repeat-love-in-the-age-of-tech)). The NNEDV Safety Net project calls for consent before tracking and visible tracking, and warns that location sharing can be dangerous in a stalker's hands ([Senate testimony 2014](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/download/06-04-14-southworth-testimony?download=1), [Safety Net Project](https://www.techsafety.org/tech-talk-location-tracking)). Levy and Schneier's "intimate threats" paper explains why standard protections fail among people close to a target ([Schneier on Security](https://www.schneier.com/academic/archives/2020/06/privacy_threats_in_i.html)). Other supporting work includes a CHI '21 abstract showing people set location sharing by intimacy level ([LMU PDF](https://www.medien.informatik.uni-muenchen.de/pubdb/publications/pub/schneegassch2021lylo/schneegassch2021lylo.pdf)) and a 2025 survey (n=434) linking monitoring scope to perceived intrusion ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-dynamics/articles/10.3389/fhumd.2025.1553619/pdf)). Apple Safety Check shows who can see your location, allows per-person stopping and offers an Emergency Reset ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/ips2aad835e1)). Survivor guidance warns that removing a tracker can alert the person tracking ([Tech and Safety 2026 (MCASA)](https://mcasa.org/assets/files/Tech_and_Safety_2026.pdf), [Technology safety plan tip sheet](https://techsafety.technicalities.ca/resources/toolkits/technology-safety-plan-tip-sheet/)). Trauma-informed computing (six principles, 22 recommendations) warns that unexpected system changes can provoke panic in survivors of abuse ([Cornell Chronicle on Chen et al., CHI '22](https://news.cornell.edu/index.php/node/324445), [Airbnb Design](https://Airbnb.design/designing-for-crisis/)).

Inference (not sourced): YTC's rule that a host cannot enable monitoring for another participant fits this literature. The host role is a power asymmetry, so the host should see only fields each participant consented to, and participants should be able to preview exactly what the host and backups would receive. Two consent layers (join the session; escalation data tier, as in Apple's model) are a natural fit. Auto-expiry directly answers the "always on" criticism.

## Wording, staleness and stress: what the guidance supports

Measurable interface anchors are few but firm. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 sets a **24x24 CSS px** minimum target ([W3C Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), and Android guidance sets **48x48 dp** with 8 dp spacing ([Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). Stress guidance says to give one task per screen, strong defaults, high-priority actions first, undo for irreversible errors and a pre-built emergency mode, and to test in noisy, busy real environments ([Smashing Magazine, Nov 2025](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/11/designing-for-stress-emergency/)). A secondary summary of UX Planet says the interface should contract under stress ([daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/designing-for-the-wrong-user-wchgrzdj8.md), [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/designing-for-the-wrong-user-33178f5cb5b8)). NN/g separates slips (autopilot errors) from mistakes and recommends constraints and good defaults ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/slips/)).

No study tests "acknowledged" versus "safe", staleness labels or last-known versus current wording in a safety context. The nearest evidence is indirect: "delivered" and "read" receipts are commonly confused ([Infobip](https://cdn-web.infobip.com/uploads/markdown-exports/www.infobip.com/blog/rcs-read-receipts.md), [moderncrypto list](https://moderncrypto.org/mail-archive/messaging/2014/000277.html)), and calibrated forecasts improved weather decisions and trust ([Joslyn et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4812), [Joslyn slides](https://www.mmm.ucar.edu/sites/default/files/2022-07/Susan%2520Joslyn%25203%2520October%2520%25202019%2520MMM%2520SEMINAR.pdf)), with inconsistency less harmful to trust than inaccuracy ([Burgeno and Joslyn](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/2606e30e-b9a8-4669-9a3f-57914fff773c/full)). These support event-literal wording only by analogy.

## Unverified items and gaps

Several sourced details are unconfirmed or conflicting. Apple Crash Detection's second-stage countdown is reported as 30 s and as 10 s. Google Safety Check durations conflict (24-hour maximum on the support page versus 1 minute to 8 hours in a blog summary), and Google's own Safety Check page returned an error. Glympse duration ranges conflict (5 minutes to 12 hours versus 15 minutes to 4 hours). ## Conclusion

The research reframes YTC's design problem. Existing apps solve "how do I tell someone I'm late or hurt", and they do it with a single user, a single deadline and a single recipient class. YTC's group, host and backup structure has no precedent in the surveyed set, so its most defensible choices are the ones that borrow Apple's discipline (consent tiers, silence-only disclosure, visible state, extension instead of reset) and extend it with distinctions no source tests: acknowledged versus safe, observed versus concluded, last-known versus current, simulated versus real.

The evidence is also a warning about where YTC is most exposed. The documented harms are false alerts that burden recipients and location sharing that turns into control. A group host holds both risks, so the practical priority is not more features but legible limits: one alert per escalation step, a consent ledger participants can read, expiry that is stated plainly, and a demo label no one can miss. The first user tests should check whether impaired or stressed people can read these states, since no source in the notes answers that.
